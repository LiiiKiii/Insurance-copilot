Claims and Policy FAQ (2026 Edition)
Applicable products: all personal insurance products of China Life (Overseas)
Version: January 2026 | Claims Hotline: (852) 2287-0000

Q1: How long does it generally take for the insurance company to settle a claim?
A1: For cases with complete documentation, inpatient and surgical claims are generally approved within 7 to 14 working days, and critical illness and death claims within 30 working days. If additional medical reports are required, the time will be extended accordingly. Clients can check the approval progress at any time through their agent or the Client App.

Q2: What documents are required for a critical illness claim?
A2: The following must be submitted: (1) a completed claim form; (2) a medical certificate issued by the attending doctor; (3) relevant pathological and laboratory reports, e.g. a biopsy report for cancer; (4) a copy of the insured's identity document; (5) the policyholder's bank account details for claim payment. Death claims additionally require the death certificate and estate administration documents.

Q3: Is medical treatment received outside Hong Kong covered by the medical insurance?
A3: The Standard plan covers Hong Kong and Macau; the Upgrade plan covers designated regions in Asia; the Global Premier plan covers worldwide (excluding sanctioned regions). For overseas treatment, keep the original itemised hospital bills, doctor's reports and receipts, and submit them within 90 days of returning to Hong Kong. The Global plan provides dedicated cover for emergency medical evacuation expenses.

Q4: What if a condition is diagnosed during the waiting period?
A4: The waiting period for critical illness insurance is 90 days from the policy effective date. If a covered critical illness is first diagnosed within the waiting period, the insurance company has the right to decline the claim, refund the premiums paid and terminate the policy; however, conditions directly caused by accidents are not subject to the waiting period. The policy should therefore be incepted as early as possible after application - do not delay the effective date.

Q5: What happens if a client forgets to declare pre-existing conditions at application?
A5: Hong Kong insurance operates on the principle of utmost good faith. If it is proven that the client deliberately concealed material facts at application (e.g. diagnosed cancer or a history of diabetes), the insurance company has the right to rescind the policy and decline claims within the contestability period (generally two years). Agents are obliged to guide clients to answer the health questionnaire truthfully and must not instigate or imply concealment.

Q6: How are the days of hospital cash allowance calculated?
A6: Counting starts from 00:00 on the first day of hospitalisation and is based on consecutive days in hospital; the day of discharge is not counted. Re-admission for the same condition within 90 days of discharge is treated as the same hospitalisation; re-admission for a different condition or after more than 90 days counts as a new hospitalisation, with a maximum of 365 compensated days per year.

Q7: Can a policy be reinstated after it has lapsed?
A7: The grace period for premium payment is 31 days after the due date, during which cover remains in force. After the grace period expires the policy lapses; the client may apply for reinstatement within 24 months from the lapse date. All overdue premiums and interest must be paid, and a new health declaration submitted; the policy is restored only after underwriting approval. The waiting period is recalculated after reinstatement.

Q8: Can a client claim compensation if the non-guaranteed dividends of an annuity plan fall short of expectations?
A8: No. Non-guaranteed dividends are declared by the insurance company based on investment performance and are not guaranteed amounts; the projections in the plan illustration are for reference only. Only the guaranteed cash value and guaranteed annuity are contractual obligations. Agents must explain both the guaranteed and non-guaranteed components when selling and retain the sales records.

Q9: Does the medical insurance conflict with the client's company group insurance?
A9: No. Medical reimbursement insurance follows the principle of indemnity - the same medical expense cannot be claimed twice. The client may first claim from the group insurance and then apply to the personal medical insurance for the balance, using the "Settlement Statement" as proof of receipt. Hospital cash allowance is a fixed daily benefit and is not subject to the indemnity principle.

Q10: What procedures are required to change the beneficiary?
A10: The policyholder may apply in writing at any time to change the beneficiary by completing the "Beneficiary Change Form" and returning it to the insurance company; the beneficiary's signature is not required. If the policy is held under a trust arrangement or has been assigned as security, consent from the relevant parties must be obtained before any change. Clients are advised to review their beneficiary arrangements in a timely manner after marriage, divorce or the birth of a child.

Q11: What are the requirements for claiming a tax deduction on Voluntary Health Insurance Scheme (VHIS) products?
A11: For recognised VHIS products, each insured person may claim a premium deduction of up to HKD 8,000 per tax assessment year. Taxpayers may take out policies for themselves and specified relatives; the policy must be a recognised product issued by a recognised insurer. When filing tax returns, clients only need to keep the policy and premium receipts - no separate proof needs to be submitted, but the records should be retained for 6 years for inspection.

Q12: What appeal channels are available after a claim is rejected?
A12: The client may first ask the insurance company for a written reason for the rejection and submit supplementary information within 14 days to request a review; if still dissatisfied, they may complain to the Insurance Complaints Bureau (ICB), where eligible cases may receive a free independent ruling, with a maximum compensation of HKD 1,000,000 (for personal injury and other claims). Agents should help clients prepare the materials rather than discourage appeals.

Q13: How is the cooling-off period of a policy calculated?
A13: Within 21 calendar days of receiving the policy and signing the cooling-off notice, the client may apply unconditionally to cancel the policy, and the insurance company will refund all premiums paid (if the policy currency has depreciated, an exchange difference may be deducted based on market value). The cooling-off period is a statutory protection and must be brought to the client's attention verbally and in writing at the time of sale.

Q14: How can family members enquire about a client's policy after the client's death?
A14: Family members may enquire about the policy through the agent or the claims hotline with the death certificate, proof of relationship and their own identity document. If the policy number is unknown, the insurance company can search using the insured's name and identity card number. Where probate is involved, the claim proceeds may need to be paid to the estate administrator first.
