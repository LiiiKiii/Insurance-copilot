Underwriting Guidelines Manual (2026 Edition)
Document No.: UW-2026-01 | Scope: Individual applications for life, critical illness and medical products | Effective date: 1 January 2026

Chapter 1 General Underwriting Principles
The purpose of underwriting is to assess risk and ensure that premiums are commensurate with risk. All applications must be assessed across four dimensions: health declaration, occupation, financial status and lifestyle. Agents must record client information truthfully, must not answer health questions on behalf of clients, and must not modify questionnaire content without the client's authorisation.
Underwriting decisions fall into five categories: standard acceptance, acceptance with a premium loading, acceptance with exclusions, deferral and declinature. Any non-standard decision will be communicated to the client in writing with the reasons stated.

Chapter 2 Body Mass Index (BMI) and Medical Examination Requirements
BMI is calculated as weight (kg) divided by the square of height (m). The medical examination requirements for each age and sum assured combination are as follows:
Issue age | Sum assured (HKD) | Medical examination requirements
0 to 40 | Below 5,000,000 | None required; health questionnaire
41 to 50 | Below 3,000,000 | None required; blood and urine tests for 3,000,000 and above
51 to 60 | Below 1,000,000 | Simplified medical examination; full medical examination for 1,000,000 and above
61 and above | Any sum assured | Full medical examination and ECG
A BMI between 28 and 32 generally attracts a premium loading of 25% to 50%; a BMI above 32 must be referred to the reinsurer for assessment and may require blood glucose, lipid and liver function tests.

Chapter 3 Smoking and Lifestyle
Applicants who have used any tobacco product (including e-cigarettes) within the 12 months before application are underwritten at smoker rates, which are generally 15% to 25% higher than non-smoker rates. A client who has quit smoking for a full 12 months after the policy takes effect may apply to switch to non-smoker rates and must submit a nicotine test (urine or saliva).
Applicants who regularly take part in high-risk activities such as diving, paragliding, motor racing or combat sports must complete a sports and leisure questionnaire; the underwriting department will decide on a premium loading or exclusion clause based on the frequency and level of the activity.

Chapter 4 Common Underwriting Outcomes for Medical Conditions
Health condition | Typical underwriting outcome | Required documents
Hypertension (well controlled) | Standard or loading of 10% to 20% | Blood pressure records from the last 3 follow-ups, ECG
Type 2 diabetes | Life loading from 50%; critical illness loading or exclusion | HbA1c, renal function and fundus examination reports
Thyroid nodule (benign) | Standard if biopsy is benign | Ultrasound report, fine-needle aspiration report
Fatty liver (mild) | Generally standard if liver function is normal | Liver enzyme test, liver ultrasound report
History of depression or anxiety | Deferral or exclusion depending on severity | Psychiatrist's report, proof of discontinuation of medication and follow-up
Pregnancy | Generally standard before week 28 | Antenatal records; may be reassessed 6 weeks after delivery

Chapter 5 Financial Underwriting
When the sum assured applied for exceeds "annual income x 15" or the death benefit exceeds HKD 10,000,000, financial proof must be submitted, including tax returns, MPF contribution records, bank statements or audited company reports. If the client has existing policies, the total sum assured should be aggregated to determine whether there is over-insurance or moral hazard.
Large policies require a completed financial needs analysis (FNA) explaining the relationship between the sum assured and the client's family liabilities, income and future responsibilities; the documents must be signed by the client and retained for 7 years.

Chapter 6 Approval Timelines and Review
Standard cases are issued within 3 working days once the documentation is complete; cases requiring a medical examination or medical reports generally take 10 to 15 working days; cases involving reinsurance may be extended to 20 working days. If a client disputes the underwriting outcome, they may submit supplementary information through their agent within 30 days to request a review, which is handled by a senior underwriting officer.

Compliance note: This manual is an internal underwriting reference; the actual outcome is subject to the formal approval of the underwriting department. Agents must not promise clients that they will be "guaranteed standard acceptance" or advise clients to conceal their medical history.
